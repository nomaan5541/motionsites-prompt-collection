## 2. Four-Factor Fair Use Analysis

In accordance with judicial guidelines for evaluating fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107:

### Factor 1: The Purpose and Character of the Use
- **Transformative Nature**: The materials here are restructured into standardized, machine-readable natural language prompts designed to instruct Large Language Models (LLMs) on visual layout composition, CSS hierarchy, and responsive styling. They do not substitute for the original works.
- **Non-Commercial Open Source**: The entire repository is published openly under the MIT License without paywalls, subscription fees, or commercial gatekeeping.

### Factor 2: The Nature of the Copyrighted Work
- The prompts describe functional UI layouts, geometric alignments, color palettes, and component hierarchy — concepts that fall under functional and structural design patterns rather than purely expressive literary or artistic works.

### Factor 3: The Amount and Substantiality of the Portion Used
- Only the minimal necessary descriptive metadata, structural layout instructions, and low-resolution preview thumbnails needed to evaluate and reconstruct the design pattern are included.

### Factor 4: The Effect of the Use upon the Potential Market
- This open-source research collection has no adverse effect on the market for original commercial web applications. In fact, it provides attribution, references, and visibility for contemporary web design standards.
